A field trip with a shared question

How are Spain’s histories, identities and connections represented through language, heritage and cultural mediation? Explore museums, neighbourhoods and historic places, collect evidence and compare different ways of telling their stories.

The programme combines preparation before departure, guided and independent fieldwork in Spain, and work after the trip. Arrival and transfer stages carry a lighter academic load.

SAFETY & RESPONSIBILITY

Stay connected and prepared

Attend the safety briefing, follow agreed communication and check-in arrangements, and keep trip emergency information with you.

Stay reachable

  • Keep your phone charged and mobile data available.
  • Use the channel agreed by the coordinators.
  • Know the next meeting point and time.
  • Tell the teacher promptly if you are unwell, delayed or need help.

Move with care

  • Follow instructions for visits, transport and group movements.
  • Move with others during independent time and avoid travelling alone at night.
  • Allow for heat, walking distances and fatigue.
  • Respect communities, access rules and the approved code of conduct.

Protect belongings

  • Keep passports, money, cards and devices secure.
  • Take care in busy places and on public transport.
  • Keep separate, secure copies of essential information.
  • Allow for personal purchases and costs caused by lost items or missed services.
If something happens: make the situation safe, contact the appropriate emergency service if needed and inform the accompanying teacher. For urgent emergency assistance in Spain, call 112. See useful numbers and the call script ↓

ACADEMIC DESIGN & ASSESSMENT

Connect observations with a question

The proposed six-credit course combines preparation, situated learning and synthesis. Detailed requirements and rubrics remain subject to approval.

BEFORE DEPARTURE

Prepare a question

Explore historical contexts, comparative area-studies concepts, methods and ethics. Research a site or theme and formulate a question for the trip.

IN SPAIN

Gather evidence

Use briefings, observation records, interpretative materials and debriefings to connect visits. The portfolio draws on evidence from at least two visits.

AFTER THE TRIP

Interpret and communicate

Develop a team presentation or mediation project and an individual analytical report. Connect a Spanish case with a regional or global issue.

FIELD TASKS

Observe, document, compare, create

Short, purposeful tasks connect places with course themes. These examples show the approach; final briefs will specify evidence and outputs.

01 · HERITAGE INTERPRETATION

Read a place for different audiences

Compare signs, displays or explanations at the Alhambra or another site. What is selected, translated or left unexplained?

Collect: observation notes, a permitted photograph and a short comparison of two interpretative choices.
02 · URBAN LANGUAGE

Notice everyday communication

Observe publicly visible language in streets, markets and neighbourhoods. Consider migration, commerce, tourism and urban change.

Collect: examples with location and context. Interviews and Spanish-language interactions are not required.
03 · CULTURAL MEDIATION

Explain a work for your audience

Consider how a work at the Prado or another museum is contextualised. Develop an alternative explanation for students in Hong Kong.

Create: a caption, audio guide, visual resource or other team output grounded in evidence.
04 · COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS

Bring two places into conversation

Compare representations of diversity or memory across sites. Connect the Spanish case with wider questions and an Asian comparison where relevant.

Develop: a clear question, selected evidence and an analytical interpretation.

A useful evidence record

  • Name the place and record date and context.
  • Separate your notes from quotations and identify sources.
  • Caption images and explain why they matter.
  • Back up notes and media regularly.
  • Connect evidence from more than one visit.

Collect evidence responsibly

  • Follow site rules for photography and recording.
  • Ask permission before photographing or recording people.
  • Focus on public observation and avoid unnecessary personal information.
  • Use supported Spanish where helpful. Academic work can be done in English if you lack proficiency in Spanish.
  • Bring questions and uncertainties to debriefings.

EMERGENCIES

Know who to call and what to say

Keep these essentials available offline. Teacher contacts, accommodation details and insurance instructions will be provided through the course before departure.

112Emergency assistance in Spain

For urgent emergency assistance, call 112, give your location and follow the operator’s instructions. Then inform the accompanying teacher.

Call 112

Useful public numbers

Use 112 if you are unsure which emergency service is needed. Specialist numbers and coverage are explained in the linked official sources.

What to say when you call

  1. Identify yourself. Give your name and say you are a university student visiting from Hong Kong.
  2. Give your location. State the city, exact address or clear landmark. Share the location on your phone if asked.
  3. Explain the situation. Say what happened, how many people need help and whether anyone is injured.
  4. Give a contact number. Keep the line available and follow instructions.
  5. Inform the teacher. Once emergency assistance has been contacted, alert the accompanying teacher through the agreed channel.
A useful opening in Spanish

“Necesito ayuda. Estoy en… Mi nombre es… Mi número de teléfono es…”

I need help. I am at… My name is… My telephone number is…
Before departure: save the verified teacher contact, accommodation addresses, insurance assistance details and University emergency procedure. Personal and medical records are managed through authorised institutional channels, not this public site.
